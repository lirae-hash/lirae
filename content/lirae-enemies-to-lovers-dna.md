# LIRAE — Story DNA
## Trope: Enemies-to-Lovers (Contemporary)
### Version 1.0 — Internal Working Document

---

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

This DNA document defines the fixed emotional skeleton of every enemies-to-lovers adventure in Lirae. It never changes. What changes per adventure is the **Setting Sheet** — a short companion document that fills every `[SLOT]` marked below.

When generating a chapter, the AI receives:
- The relevant beat from this document (fixed rules + emotional function)
- The filled slots from the Setting Sheet (world-specific details)
- The reader's spice level (1–5)
- The reader's vibe (Dark Desire / Golden Hour / Midnight Rose / Emerald Envy)
- Any choices the reader made in previous chapters

---

## VOICE RULES (apply to every chapter, every adventure)

**POV:** Close third person. Stay in her head. We feel everything she feels, including things she won't admit to herself.

**Tense:** Past tense.

**Her character (fixed across all adventures):**
She is intelligent, competent, and privately tired of having to prove it. She does not shrink. She is not naive. She has been underestimated before and she will not let it happen again. She is also, underneath the armor, someone who wants things very much — she just doesn't say so.

**His character (fixed across all adventures):**
Julian Voss. Controlled. Composed to the point of maddening. Not cruel — precise. He chooses his words carefully and says less than he means. He has done something that hurt her, and he knows it. Whether he regrets it is the question the adventure slowly answers.

**Tone calibration by spice level:**

| Level | Label | Prose quality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sweet warmth | Emotional tension only. Physical awareness limited to presence and voice. No body-scanning. |
| 2 | Warm tension | Charged glances. Awareness of proximity. She notices his hands, his jaw, his stillness. Restrained. |
| 3 | Slow burn | Electric. She is aware of exactly how close he is at all times. Almost-touches. Charged dialogue with subtext. |
| 4 | Simmering desire | Overt attraction she's actively resisting. Near-misses. One moment per chapter that almost breaks. |
| 5 | Scorching | Intense physical and emotional tension. Barely-contained. Her thoughts go places she immediately shuts down. |

**Prose style reference:** Write like Emily Henry or Colleen Hoover — real, adult, emotionally precise. Not YA. Not purple. Not overwrought. The restraint is what makes it hit.

**What to avoid:**
- Describing her appearance to herself in mirrors
- "She didn't know why she felt this way" (she does know)
- His eyes changing color
- Telling the reader how to feel ("it was electrifying")
- Dialogue that exists only to explain backstory

## THE 10 BEATS

---

### BEAT 1 — The Clash
**Chapter title:** "The Rival"
**Emotional function:** Establish the enemy dynamic and the attraction simultaneously. She must feel both in the same moment — the threat and the pull.

**Fixed elements (always present, regardless of setting):**
- She arrives somewhere and senses him before she sees him
- When she sees him, she recognises who he is immediately
- The power imbalance is established — he has something over her professionally
- She is shown to be formidable. She does not break. She does not flinch visibly.
- He notices her noticing him. He is not surprised to see her.
- The chapter ends before any real conversation happens — just the fact of him

**Variable slots (filled by Setting Sheet):**
- `[LOCATION]` — where this scene takes place
- `[HIS ROLE]` — what position gives him power over her
- `[HER ROLE]` — what she stands to lose
- `[WHAT HE DID]` — the history she carries in (she knows; the reader learns)
- `[THE DETAIL]` — one specific sensory detail of him that she notices and wishes she hadn't

**Hook type:** Revelation
**Hook function:** Something she learns at the end of this chapter changes the power dynamic. It's not what she expected.

**Choices at end of ch.1:**
- Choice A: Bold/direct — she initiates contact on her own terms
- Choice B: Strategic — she gathers information before moving
- Choice C: Unexpected — she does something that reveals more about her than him

---

### BEAT 2 — Forced Proximity
**Chapter title:** "No Escape"
**Emotional function:** Remove the option to avoid each other. They must now exist in close quarters. The irritation is real. So is everything else.

**Fixed elements:**
- A situation forces them into physical or professional proximity
- She tries to establish distance or rules; he does not fight it but doesn't help her either
- One moment of accidental closeness — not romantic, just physical reality
- She catches herself noticing something about him that is not his professional role
- She immediately rationalises it

**Variable slots:**
- `[PROXIMITY CATALYST]` — the trip, project, assignment, or event that traps them
- `[CLOSE QUARTERS SCENE]` — the specific moment of accidental physical closeness
- `[THE THING SHE NOTICES]` — the specific non-professional detail she registers

**Hook type:** Cliffhanger
**Hook function:** Something external cuts the chapter at the worst possible moment — the situation just got harder to escape.

**Choices at end of ch.2:**
- Choice A: She leans into the proximity — makes it her advantage
- Choice B: She establishes a clear professional boundary, out loud

---

### BEAT 3 — Reluctant Truce
**Chapter title:** "Fine"
**Emotional function:** They need each other. Both hate it. The truce is practical, not warm — but something shifts when they actually work together.

**Fixed elements:**
- A shared goal or crisis forces genuine cooperation
- He is unexpectedly good at something she respects
- She is unexpectedly good at something he needed
- They achieve something together. Neither says so.
- The chapter ends with the truce holding — but something underneath it has changed

**Variable slots:**
- `[THE SHARED GOAL]` — what they must accomplish together
- `[HIS COMPETENCE]` — the specific thing he does that earns her (reluctant) respect
- `[HER COMPETENCE]` — the specific thing she does that earns his
- `[THE MOMENT IT SHIFTS]` — a small, specific beat where the dynamic changes tone

**Hook type:** Choice
**Hook function:** The chapter ends on an offered gesture — not romantic, but the first genuinely human thing between them. The reader decides whether she accepts it.

**Choices at end of ch.3:**
- Choice A: Accept the gesture — acknowledge the shift
- Choice B: Deflect with professionalism — protect the distance

---

### BEAT 4 — First Crack
**Chapter title:** "Something Real"
**Emotional function:** A crack in his armor. She sees something beneath the composure that is not the enemy she constructed. It unsettles her more than his competence ever did.

**Fixed elements:**
- She encounters him outside his professional role, or in a moment he didn't control
- He is briefly, undeniably human
- She has a choice: exploit it or protect it. She chooses without quite deciding to.
- He knows she saw. He does not address it.
- She spends the rest of the chapter trying to un-see it

**Variable slots:**
- `[THE CRACK SCENE]` — the specific moment and circumstance of vulnerability
- `[WHAT SHE SEES]` — the specific thing that breaks her image of him
- `[HER CHOICE]` — how she responds in the moment (reader's previous choices influence this)

**Hook type:** Revelation
**Hook function:** She finds out something about his past that reframes what he did to her. Not exonerates — reframes. She is not ready for this information.

**Choices at end of ch.4:**
- Choice A: Confront the new information directly
- Choice B: File it away and say nothing — for now

---

### BEAT 5 — The Almost ★ MIDPOINT
**Chapter title:** "Almost"
**Emotional function:** The pivot of the entire adventure. The tension that has been building since ch.1 reaches a peak and breaks — but not into resolution. Into awareness. She cannot pretend anymore that this is only professional.

**Fixed elements:**
- A specific scene of forced, extended closeness — not accidental, intentional on someone's part
- The space between them becomes the subject of the scene without either of them saying so
- He moves closer. Or she does. Or neither does but both want to.
- Something specific — not an interruption, but a choice — stops it
- The chapter ends without resolution. The reader feels the exact shape of what didn't happen.

**Variable slots:**
- `[THE ALMOST SCENE]` — the specific setting and circumstance of the near-miss
- `[WHO MOVES]` — which of them closes the distance (reader's choices influence this)
- `[THE INTERRUPTION]` — what stops it (internal or external — Setting Sheet specifies)

**Spice level governs this beat more than any other:**
- Level 1–2: The almost is emotional only. A look that lasts too long. Words that mean something else.
- Level 3: Physical proximity is explicit. She is aware of every inch of the space between them.
- Level 4: He touches her — jaw, wrist, shoulder — and holds for one second longer than professional.
- Level 5: The chapter brings them to the edge. The reader feels exactly what almost happened.

**No reader choices at end of ch.5.** The beat needs to land without asking anything of the reader. She just feels it.

---

### BEAT 6 — The Setback
**Chapter title:** "What She Found"
**Emotional function:** She finds out what he did — the full shape of it. Not a rumour. Evidence. The walls go back up. This is worse than before ch.4 because now she knows what she was starting to feel.

**Fixed elements:**
- She discovers the truth of what he did without him present
- The discovery recontextualises something warm from a previous chapter — now it reads as manipulation
- She does not confront him immediately. She sits with it.
- The chapter ends before the confrontation — reader chooses how she responds

**Variable slots:**
- `[WHAT SHE FINDS]` — the specific evidence or revelation (from Setting Sheet)
- `[THE WARM THING RECONTEXTUALISED]` — which earlier moment now reads differently
- `[WHERE SHE IS WHEN SHE FINDS IT]` — the specific physical location

**Hook type:** Cliffhanger
**Hook function:** The chapter cuts right before she has to decide what to do with what she knows.

**Choices at end of ch.6:**
- Choice A: Escalate — take it to whoever has power to act on it
- Choice B: Go directly to him — alone, tonight, now

---

### BEAT 7 — The Reckoning
**Chapter title:** "Say It"
**Emotional function:** The confrontation. Everything they've been not saying since ch.1 is now in the room. This is the most emotionally naked chapter. Both of them are stripped of their professional identities here.

**Fixed elements:**
- She initiates (regardless of reader choice — this is her beat)
- He does not deflect. For the first time, he meets her directly.
- She says the accusation plainly. He does not deny it.
- Something about his response is unexpected — not what she prepared for
- The chapter ends unresolved. He has given her something to think about but not an answer.

**Variable slots:**
- `[WHERE THE CONFRONTATION HAPPENS]` — the Setting Sheet specifies a location that carries meaning in this world
- `[HIS UNEXPECTED RESPONSE]` — what he says or does that is not the denial or justification she expected

**Hook type:** Choice
**Hook function:** He's given her something. Now she has to decide whether to hear the rest.

**Choices at end of ch.7:**
- Choice A: Walk out — she's not ready
- Choice B: Stay — ask him to explain, knowing she might believe him

---

### BEAT 8 — The Confession
**Chapter title:** "What He Didn't Say"
**Emotional function:** The real reason. Not an excuse — a truth. What he did was real. But the reason behind it changes the shape of everything. She has to reckon with what she's been told.

**Fixed elements:**
- He tells her something she didn't know — a sacrifice, a cost, a protection she didn't ask for
- The revelation is specific and verifiable — not just words
- She does not immediately forgive him. She absorbs it.
- Something shifts physically in this chapter — she lets him closer than before
- The chapter ends just before the break — the reader feels the edge

**Variable slots:**
- `[THE REAL REASON]` — the specific truth behind what he did (from Setting Sheet — this is the emotional centre of the whole adventure)
- `[THE PROOF]` — the specific detail that makes it undeniable
- `[THE PHYSICAL SHIFT]` — the small, specific change in their physical proximity

**Hook type:** Revelation
**Hook function:** The last image of the chapter is something that makes the reader hold their breath.

**Choices at end of ch.8:**
- Choice A: Say nothing — close the distance
- Choice B: Say everything — all of it, out loud, first

---

### BEAT 9 — The Surrender ★ SPICE PEAK
**Chapter title:** "She Chooses"
**Emotional function:** She stops fighting it. So does he. The chapter delivers on every beat of tension built since ch.1. This is the payoff.

**Fixed elements:**
- She makes the first real move (the whole adventure has been building to her agency here)
- He matches her — he has been waiting
- The scene is grounded in the specific world of the Setting Sheet
- What happens here is shaped entirely by the spice level the reader set at the beginning

**Spice level governs the entire chapter:**
- Level 1: An honest, unguarded conversation. She says what she wants. He says what he wants. They decide together.
- Level 2: A first kiss. Real, unhurried, mutual.
- Level 3: A first kiss that turns into something more. The chapter ends at the beginning of that something.
- Level 4: The chapter goes further — rendered with enough specificity that the reader feels it, without being explicit.
- Level 5: The chapter is fully realized. Emotional and physical. The writing earns the heat.

**Variable slots:**
- `[THE SURRENDER SCENE]` — the specific setting (from Setting Sheet — this should be the most sensory-rich location in the adventure)
- `[THE FINAL CATALYST]` — the specific thing that breaks the last wall

**No choices at end of ch.9.** The reader just experiences this.

---

### BEAT 10 — The Resolution
**Chapter title:** determined by reader's choice pattern
**Emotional function:** Land the story. Honour the choices she made. Leave her with something real.

**Three possible endings — determined by reader's choice pattern:**

**HEA (Happily Ever After):** Reader consistently chose vulnerability and connection. The ending is full and forward-looking. They are together. The future is open. One specific, earned moment of joy.

**HFN (Happy For Now):** Reader made mixed choices — bold and cautious, open and defended. The ending is honest about uncertainty. They are together but the future is real, not fairy-tale. This is often the most resonant ending for 30+ readers.

**Heartbreak Ending:** Reader consistently chose distance, protection, escalation. The relationship doesn't survive — not because she was wrong to choose those things, but because those choices have weight. The ending is written with dignity and beauty. She is not diminished. She is just alone. **This ending triggers the rewind mechanic.**

**Fixed elements across all endings:**
- A closing scene that mirrors the opening of ch.1 — but different now
- One line that lands the whole adventure
- A final beat that seeds the next adventure (new question, new city, new conflict — same heat)

**Variable slots:**
- `[THE CLOSING MIRROR SCENE]` — the specific setting that echoes ch.1
- `[THE SEASON 2 SEED]` — the specific question or circumstance that opens the next adventure

## CHOICE TRACKING — how reader choices affect generation

Each choice made by the reader is appended to a **choice log** that travels with the story. When generating the next chapter, the AI receives:

```
CHOICE LOG:
Ch.1: She held his gaze and took the chair beside his.
Ch.3: She accepted the gesture.
Ch.4: She filed it away and said nothing.
Ch.6: She went directly to him, alone.
Ch.7: She stayed and asked him to explain.
Ch.8: She said nothing and closed the distance.
```

This log does two things:
1. Tells the AI which ending trajectory the reader is on (vulnerability → HEA, distance → Heartbreak, mixed → HFN)
2. Adds specific texture to each new chapter — the AI writes as if the previous choices happened

The prose doesn't recap choices mechanically. It just carries them. A reader who took the chair beside his in ch.1 gets a Julian who has been watching her slightly differently ever since.

# LIRAE — Setting Sheet
## Adventure 2: "The Kitchen"
### World: Rival chefs · Paris · Contemporary

---

**ADVENTURE TITLE:** The Kitchen
**SETTING WORLD:** Michelin-starred restaurant world, Paris
**TIME PERIOD:** Contemporary

---

## CHARACTERS

**HIS ROLE:** Executive Chef and sole owner of Le Sorel — three Michelin stars, year-long waitlists, his face on every magazine that covers food as art. He runs his kitchen with exacting precision. He is not cruel, just exact. Nothing gets past him.

**HER ROLE:** Newly hired Head Pastry Chef. Her desserts have made her reputation in London — technically extraordinary, emotionally devastating. She came to Paris because it was the best offer she had after the Lyon situation. She does not know yet that he engineered her being here.

**THEIR HISTORY:** Two years ago at a culinary competition in Lyon, he was the lead judge. She was the frontrunner. He scored her signature dish a 7 — precise enough to be defensible, low enough to cost her first place. In his published notes he wrote: *"technically impressive but emotionally empty."* The phrase followed her everywhere. She rebuilt her career around proving it wrong.

**THE REAL REASON (ch.8 revelation):** He scored her honestly as he saw it that day — but what he saw was that her work was reaching for something his couldn't. The note was self-protection dressed as criticism. He has thought about that dish every week since. He hired her because he needed to know if he was right. He already suspects he wasn't.

---

## SLOT FILLS

**Beat 1 — LOCATION:**
The tasting kitchen at Le Sorel. Her first day. Late October afternoon, the kitchen not yet lit for service.

**Beat 1 — THE DETAIL:**
His hands. She notices them before his face — the way he handles a small knife while talking to a sous chef, without looking down, the way someone does when a tool has been part of them for thirty years. She wishes she hadn't noticed.

**Beat 2 — PROXIMITY CATALYST:**
The restaurant's previous pastry chef quit four days before she arrived — a fact no one mentioned in her interview. The relaunch tasting menu goes live in three weeks. He needs her in the kitchen from 7am to midnight, every day, until it's ready. There is no professional distance in a kitchen this size.

**Beat 2 — CLOSE QUARTERS SCENE:**
The kitchen pass at 11pm after a long test service. Everyone else has gone. They're the last two plating a dessert component. She turns from the counter and his chest is five inches from her shoulder. He doesn't step back. Neither does she. He reaches past her for a spoon.

**Beat 3 — THE SHARED GOAL:**
A Michelin inspector has made an unannounced visit — word travels fast in Paris kitchens. They have four days to rebuild the dessert section of the tasting menu from scratch. He pulls her into his office and says: "Tell me what you need." It is the first time he's asked her anything.

**Beat 3 — THE MOMENT IT SHIFTS:**
Night two, 2am. She's made something new — not on the plan, just an instinct. He walks through, stops, picks up the spoon without asking. Tastes it. Closes his eyes for three full seconds. Sets the spoon down and walks out without saying a word. She watches him go. That silence is the first honest thing between them.

**Beat 4 — THE CRACK SCENE:**
She arrives at 6am to find him in the walk-in cold room, sitting on an overturned milk crate, still in his street clothes, staring at nothing. He doesn't hear her come in. She has never seen him not in motion before. She backs out without speaking. He never mentions it.

**Beat 5 — THE ALMOST SCENE:**
The inspector's dinner is finished. The review will be good — she knows it from the way the room felt. The kitchen empties in twenty minutes, everyone heading to the bar two streets over. He doesn't go. She doesn't go. He opens a bottle she's never seen on the shelf — something old, something he's been saving — and pours two glasses at the pass. The kitchen lights are down to half. He says her name. Her first name. Not Chef. She realizes it's the first time she's heard it in his voice.

**Beat 5 — THE INTERRUPTION:**
His phone. His business partner calling from New York. His face closes like a shutter. He sets the glass down, picks up the call, turns a quarter away. She looks at the wine for a moment. Then she picks up her coat and her bag and walks out, quietly, while he's still talking.

**Beat 6 — WHAT SHE FINDS:**
A printed email in the office printer tray — she only sees it because she's looking for paper. His business partner, written two months before she was hired: *"Are you sure about the Lyon woman? It'll be complicated."* His reply: *"She'll either prove me right or prove me wrong. Either way I want her in my kitchen to find out."*

**Beat 6 — WHERE SHE READS IT:**
Standing at the printer. The kitchen is loud thirty feet away. She reads it three times. She puts it back exactly where she found it and finishes her shift.

**Beat 7 — WHERE THE CONFRONTATION HAPPENS:**
The roof of the building at 5am, after the worst service of the month. A supplier failure, a disaster mid-course, a table that complained. Both of them are standing in the cold with nowhere left to perform. Paris is pale below them. She says: *"The Lyon email. The printer."* He doesn't deny it.

**Beat 8 — THE PROOF:**
He takes her to Lyon. To the competition archive — they keep recordings. She watches herself plate the dish on a laptop screen in a café. Then he turns the screen toward himself and watches his own face while he tasted it. She sees the moment in the recording when his expression changes. He doesn't explain it. He says: *"I was afraid of you. I've been afraid of you since the first mouthful. I wrote the only thing that would make me feel less afraid."*

**Beat 8 — THE PHYSICAL SHIFT:**
On the train back to Paris, she falls asleep. When she wakes up, her head is on his shoulder. He hasn't moved.

**Beat 9 — THE SURRENDER SCENE:**
Her section of the kitchen. Midnight. The restaurant is dark. She's finishing the last component of a new dish — the first one at Le Sorel that is entirely hers, that she designed from nothing. He comes in through the back. She doesn't hear him until he's at the counter. "You're still here," she says. "So are you," he says. She pushes the plate toward him.

**Beat 9 — THE FINAL CATALYST:**
He tastes it. He sets the spoon down. He looks at her. He says: *"This is the dish I should have given a ten."* She turns around fully. He is very close. She says: *"I know."*

**Beat 10 — THE CLOSING MIRROR SCENE:**
The tasting kitchen, late October afternoon. One year later. The same low light she walked into on her first day. The same steel and quiet. She is putting on her apron. He walks in, stops, looks at her the way he looked at her that first day — but different now. She knows what he sees.

**Beat 10 — THE SEASON 2 SEED:**
Le Sorel has been offered a location in Tokyo. He has accepted. He is going in March. He asks her across the pass, very simply: *"Bring your knives or stay. But tell me now, because I need to know before I book two seats."*

## VIBE NOTES

**Dark Desire:** The kitchen after service — industrial steel, low light, shared exhaustion. The intimacy of two people who have spent twelve hours doing something hard together.

**Golden Hour:** Paris visible through high windows. Morning light through the kitchen's small skylight during the first hour. The warmth of a place that runs on discipline and love simultaneously.

**Midnight Rose:** The pastry section specifically — the sensory world of sugar, cream, dark chocolate, cold marble. The way a dessert is always a form of seduction if you're paying attention.

**Emerald Envy:** French culinary tradition as weight and legacy. The way food here connects to land and season and memory. The lushness of produce, the earthy richness of a kitchen that takes terroir seriously.

## GENERATION NOTES FOR AI

When generating chapters for this adventure:

- The kitchen is a character. Use it specifically — the sounds, the heat, the hierarchy, the rhythm of service.
- Her world is pastry: cold marble, precision, patience, sweetness with depth. His world is savoury: heat, speed, intensity, controlled chaos.
- The professional tension is the tension — a kitchen has a clear hierarchy and she is inside his. This makes every interaction between them charged with more than personal history.
- His Lyon note ("technically impressive but emotionally empty") should echo across the adventure and be directly reversed by what she makes in ch.9.
- Paris should feel lived-in, not postcard. Rain on cobblestones. The smell of the morning market. Kitchens that look nothing like the dining rooms above them.
